3Chain reaction

When one household is hit, the whole building feels it

Suppose most of the work of Sam in customer support and Priya the accountant goes to AI.

The company needs fewer people

Sam and Priya lose their jobs

The diner loses business and lets its helper go

Everyone spends less; mortgages get tight

Banks tighten lending; more shops close

Building fees go unpaid; the elevator can’t be fixed

Eight dominoes are set up. The first one is AI.

6Who can do what

Who flips these two switches?

No one can flip them alone. Swipe through the cards to see what each can do.

  1. Don’t wait for disaster to help

    When income drops, support should kick in, without people first having to prove which AI replaced them.

  2. Share AI’s gains with every household

    Public funds, dividends and better public services are all worth discussing. Where the money comes from and how it is shared should be open and accountable.

  3. Keep essentials within reach

    Cash alone isn’t enough. If housing, health care and elder care fall short, rent and rising prices will eat it up.

  1. Know why customers still pay

    Staff learning AI doesn’t mean the business still works. Whatever customers can do themselves, they will eventually stop paying for.

  2. Say it early, decide together

    Tell employees early what is coming, and work out new roles, shorter hours and profit-sharing together.

  3. If you can’t go on, exit in order

    Pay wages in full and hand over customers first. Don’t vanish overnight.

  1. The more critical, the easier to stop

    Actions like moving money, controlling equipment or changing systems must be possible to halt and undo at any time.

  2. Settle responsibility before launch

    Have incident reporting and a way to compensate. Being able to pay for damage doesn’t make every risk acceptable.

  3. Let users move and leave

    Data should be exportable, and switching providers shouldn’t leave anyone stuck. No whole industry should depend on a single company.

  1. Report incidents to each other

    An AI incident in one place can soon show up elsewhere. Reporting quickly lets everyone patch the holes together.

  2. Don’t race to be the least guarded

    When everyone fears falling behind, everyone speeds up. Shared rules let all sides dare to slow down a little.

  3. Get the tools everywhere

    If tools are cheap and portable, places that live on outsourcing and manufacturing get a chance to build new livelihoods with AI.

  1. Learn to work with AI

    It can let one person do what used to take several, and help you try new sources of income.

  2. Slow down on hard-to-reverse decisions

    Big loans, long contracts, years spent learning a skill “AI can’t do”: test in small steps before you commit.

  3. Don’t go it alone

    Keep a few months of living costs as a cushion. Team up with peers and neighbors to find work, negotiate pay, and have a say in the rules.

7The farther future

What if, one day, AI has feelings of its own?

People may use technology to change their bodies and minds, and AI may stop being just a tool. The line between “human” and “machine” may blur.

Unenhanced peoplePeople who enhanced themselvesAI that may have feelings
  1. People shouldn’t have to prove “AI can never do my job” to deserve a future.A job can be replaced; a person’s life is still worth living.
  2. Being more capable doesn’t give anyone the right to decide for others.That holds for people. If AI truly comes to have feelings, it holds for AI too.
  3. The future need not have only one way to live.Those who want to change themselves can, and those who want to stay as they are can too.

This is a direction, not a prophecy. It won’t arrive by itself as AI grows stronger; it takes different kinds of beings recognizing and caring for one another.

8Your turn

Three questions worth asking now

  1. 1Which household in the building are you?

    Which of your tasks is hardest to hand to AI? If it were all that was left, could it support your income?

  2. 2If your income stopped tomorrow, how many months could you last?

    What about your family? Are there neighbors in the building who would help each other out?

  3. 3Who do you want to set the building’s rules?

    Companies, governments, experts, or every affected household? Where do you get a say?
